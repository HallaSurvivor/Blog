People entering category theory often have a difficult time 
because categories show up in so many guises and generalizing 
so many previous notions that it picks up terminology from 
lots and lots of areas. These different choices of terminology 
all come loaded with intuition-carrying baggage that, if you
don't have a background in the relevant subject, or if you don't 
know how a category generalizes that subject, can be hard to understand.


This is a series (in at least 2 parts) about how you might want to think of 
categories from a variety of perspectives. We'll explain some terminology, 
etc.

In this post we'll lay some groundwork, introduce 3 "sizes" of category, 
all of which behave differently. When we start talking about sources of 
intuition, we'll need to know when we can or can't apply that intuition, 
and one litmus test for how a category will behavce comes from how "big" 
it is. (Nothing to do with universes/set theoretic size issues)

1. Combinatorial Categories
(I know "combinatorial category" already has been used for other things. 
I'm open to other names.)
